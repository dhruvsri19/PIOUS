Population, sample and statistical modelling

1. A population in statistics refers to:

  1. Only the observations selected for analysis
  2. The entire collection of objects or outcomes about which information is sought
  3. Only numerical observations
  4. The mean of a data set
Answer: B. The entire collection of objects or outcomes about which information is sought

2. A sample is:

  1. The entire population
  2. A subset of the population that is actually observed
  3. A parameter of the population
  4. A probability distribution
Answer: B. A subset of the population that is actually observed

3. Which of the following is a tangible population?

  1. All possible future measurements from a chemical process
  2. All possible readings produced by a weighing instrument
  3. All 10,000 bolts currently stored in a warehouse
  4. All possible outcomes of repeatedly measuring a physical quantity
Answer: C. All 10,000 bolts currently stored in a warehouse

4. A population consisting of all values that could possibly be observed from a process under identical conditions is called:

  1. Tangible population
  2. Finite population
  3. Conceptual population
  4. Sample population 1
Answer: C. Conceptual population

5. Why is a sample often used instead of studying the entire population?

  1. A sample always gives the exact population parameters
  2. Studying the entire population is often impractical or expensive
  3. Samples contain no variation
  4. Samples eliminate uncertainty completely
Answer: B. Studying the entire population is often impractical or expensive

6. Sampling variation refers to:

  1. Errors caused by incorrect calculations
  2. Differences between different samples drawn from the same population
  3. Changes in the population definition
  4. Changes in measurement units
Answer: B. Differences between different samples drawn from the same population

7. Which statement about statistical modelling is correct?

  1. Statistics completely eliminates uncertainty
  2. Statistical methods use sample information to make inferences about populations
  3. A sample must always equal the population
  4. Statistical models are only used for deterministic data
Answer: B. Statistical methods use sample information to make inferences about populations

Sampling methods

8. In a simple random sample of size n:

  1. Every individual must be selected
  2. Every possible collection of n population items is equally likely to be selected
  3. Only the largest observations are selected

  1. Convenience sampling

  1. The sample is too small

11. A sample selected simply because the observations are easy to obtain is called:

  1. Simple random sample
  2. Stratified sample
  3. Sample of convenience

Answer: C. Sample of convenience

  1. Divided into subpopulations and a random sample is drawn from each
  2. Divided into random clusters and one observation is selected
  3. Ordered according to magnitude

Answer: A. Divided into subpopulations and a random sample is drawn from each

13. Cluster sampling involves:

  1. Selecting individuals completely at random from the entire population
  2. Selecting groups or clusters of items from the population
  3. Selecting only extreme observations
  4. Selecting observations based on convenience
Answer: B. Selecting groups or clusters of items from the population

14. When sampling without replacement from a very large finite population, sample observations can often be treated as:

  1. Dependent
  2. Independent
  3. Identical to the population

Answer: B. Independent
Approximately independent when the sampling fraction is small.

  1. 0.0087
  2. 0.0174
  3. 0.4913

Answer: No valid listed option — see correction below.
No listed option is correct for the stated data: z = 2.3570 and one-sided P ≈ 0.00921. A (0.0087) is the nearest listed option, but is not the calculated answer.

2. A sample of 50 copper wires had a mean resistance of 1.03 mΩ with a standard deviation of 0.1 mΩ. Let μ represent the mean resistance of copper wires of this type. The null and alternative hypotheses for testing whether the mean resistance is greater than 1 mΩ are:

  2. H₀: μ ≤ 1, H₁: μ > 1
  3. H₀: μ ≥ 1, H₁: μ < 1

3. A sample of 65 electric motors had a mean efficiency of 0.595 with a standard deviation of 0.05. To test H₀: μ ≥ 0.6 versus H₁: μ < 0.6, the test statistic is:

  1. z = (0.595 − 0.6)/(0.05/√65) = −0.806
  2. z = (0.6 − 0.595)/(0.05/√65) = 0.806
  3. t = (0.595 − 0.6)/(0.05/√65) = −0.806

A is the intended large-sample z-test answer in this section. Since the standard deviation is estimated, an exact normal-population t-test would use C with 64 df; both have the same numeric statistic.

z = 2.5, so two-sided P ≈ 0.01242. The portal concatenated all four options into option A; separated here.

20. A sample of 10 measurements has a mean of 55 and a standard deviation of 6. To test H₀: μ = 60 versus H₁: μ < 60, the test statistic is:

  1. t = (55 − 60)/(6/√10) = −2.635
  2. t = (60 − 55)/(6/√10) = 2.635
  3. z = (55 − 60)/(6/√10) = −2.635
  4. t = (55 − 60)/6 = −0.833
Answer: A. t = (55 − 60)/(6/√10) = −2.635

21. A small-sample hypothesis test for a population mean uses which distribution?

  1. Normal (z) distribution
  2. Student’s t distribution with n − 1 degrees of freedom
  3. Chi-square distribution
  4. F distribution
Answer: B. Student’s t distribution with n − 1 degrees of freedom
For the standard one-sample test with unknown population variance and approximately normal population.

ANOVA

22. In a one-way ANOVA, the total variation in the data is partitioned into which two components?

  1. Within-group variation and between-group variation
  2. Type I variation and Type II variation
  3. Explained variation and residual variation only
  4. Sample variation and population variation
Answer: A. Within-group variation and between-group variation
C can also describe the same decomposition when “explained” means between-group and “residual” means within-group. A is the standard ANOVA terminology and intended answer.

23. Three different machines are compared for mean tensile strength. Five wires are sampled per machine. ANOVA table: Between SS = 120, df = 2, MS = 60; Within SS = 240, df = 12, MS = 20; Total SS = 360, df = 14. What is F?

  1. 0.33
  2. 3.00
  3. 6.00
  4. 12.00
Answer: B. 3.00
F = MS_between/MS_within = 60/20 = 3.

24. A one-way ANOVA has k = 4 treatment groups and total sample size N = 32. What are the between-groups and within-groups degrees of freedom, respectively?

  1. 4 and 32
  2. 3 and 28
  3. 4 and 28
  4. 3 and 32
Answer: B. 3 and 28

Chi-square goodness of fit

  2. 4.500
  3. 6.120
  4. 10.441
Answer: A. 2.133
Expected counts = 20,140,30,10; χ² = 2.13333.

  2. 4.50
  3. 6.75
  4. 9.00
Answer: B. 4.50

28. A die is rolled 600 times. Observed frequencies for faces 1–6 are 119,99,101,86,57,110. What is the expected frequency for each face under the null hypothesis that the die is fair?

  1. 50
  2. 100
  3. 120
  4. 600
Answer: B. 100
The listed observed counts sum to 572, not 600. B follows the stated sample size of 600; if the listed counts are the complete data, expected counts would be 572/6 ≈ 95.333, which is not offered.

29. In a chi-square goodness-of-fit test with 5 categories and a sample size of 100, what are the degrees of freedom?

  2. 4
  3. 99
  4. 100
Answer: B. 4
Assuming no distribution parameters were estimated from the sample.

30. Mega Millions lottery: 15 balls are grouped into 5 equal categories, 1–3,4–6,7–9,10–12,13–15. Observed frequencies are 73,64,74,62,71. The chi-square statistic for testing fairness is:

n = 344; each expected count = 68.8; χ² ≈ 1.72674.

Chi-square independence and homogeneity

31. Four machines manufacture steel pins. Columns: Too Thin, OK, Too Thick, Total. Machine 1: 10,108,12,120; Machine 2: 34,165,2,200; Machine 3: 12,79,9,100; Machine 4: 10,60,10,80; Total: 66,403,25,500. What is the expected frequency for Machine 2—Too Thin?

  1. 26.40
  2. 34.00
  3. 15.84
  4. 10.56
Answer: A. 26.40
Using the printed margins, E = 200×66/500 = 26.4. The source table is inconsistent: Machine 1 cells sum to 130, Machine 2 to 201, and the Too Thick column sums to 33. Using only the body cells gives n = 511 and E ≈ 25.961, not an offered answer.

32. In a chi-square test for independence, a contingency table has 4 rows and 3 columns. What are the degrees of freedom?

  2. Do not reject H₀; there is no evidence that the loci are not in linkage equilibrium

Fixed-level testing and Type I / II errors

37. A Type II error is made when:

  1. H₀ is true and we reject it
  2. H₀ is false and we reject it
  3. H₀ is false and we fail to reject it
  4. H₀ is true and we fail to reject it
Answer: C. H₀ is false and we fail to reject it

38. A fixed-level test is performed at α = 0.05. If the true population mean is exactly μ₀, what is the maximum probability of rejecting H₀?

  1. 0
  2. 0.05
  3. 0.10
  4. 0.95
Answer: B. 0.05

39. A hypothesis test is performed at the 1% level. If the test statistic falls in the rejection region, the conclusion is:

  1. H₀ is rejected at the 1% level
  2. H₀ is not rejected at the 1% level
  3. H₀ is rejected at the 5% level but not at the 1% level
  4. The test is inconclusive
Answer: A. H₀ is rejected at the 1% level

40. A Type I error is made when:

  1. H₀ is true and we fail to reject it
  2. H₀ is false and we reject it
  3. H₀ is true and we reject it
  4. H₀ is false and we fail to reject it
Answer: C. H₀ is true and we reject it

41. A quality control inspector tests whether the mean diameter of bolts is 5 mm: H₀: μ = 5 versus H₁: μ ≠ 5. A Type I error means:

  1. Concluding the process is out of calibration when it is actually calibrated
  2. Concluding the process is calibrated when it is actually out of calibration
  3. Failing to detect a shift in the mean
  4. Using the wrong sample size
Answer: A. Concluding the process is out of calibration when it is actually calibrated

42. In the context of the preceding bolt-calibration question, a Type II error would mean:

  1. Concluding the process is out of calibration when it is calibrated
  2. Concluding the process is calibrated when it is actually out of calibration
  3. Correctly detecting a shift in the mean
  4. Increasing the sample size unnecessarily
Answer: B. Concluding the process is calibrated when it is actually out of calibration
More precisely, failing to detect that the process is out of calibration. The source concatenated all options into A; separated here.

43. A test is conducted at the 5% level with H₀: μ = 100 versus H₁: μ > 100. Population standard deviation σ = 15 and n = 36. The rejection region is:

  2. x̄ > 105.0

Power of a test

47. If the power of a test is 0.85, what is the probability of a Type II error?

  1. 0.85
  2. 0.15
  3. 0.05
  4. 0.95
Answer: B. 0.15

48. If the sample size is increased while the significance level remains the same, the power of the test will:

  1. Increase
  2. Decrease
  3. Stay the same
  4. Cannot be determined
Answer: A. Increase
For the same test design, variability and fixed alternative effect.

49. A test of H₀: μ = 50 versus H₁: μ > 50 has power 0.90 when μ = 55 and σ = 10. If the sample size is increased, the power at μ = 55 will:

  1. Increase above 0.90
  2. Decrease below 0.90
  3. Remain exactly 0.90
  4. Cannot be determined
Answer: A. Increase above 0.90

50. A researcher wants to increase the power of a test from 0.70 to 0.90. Which is the most effective way?

  1. Decrease the sample size
  2. Increase the sample size
  3. Decrease the significance level α
  4. Use a two-tailed test instead of a one-tailed test
Answer: B. Increase the sample size
The source concatenated all options into A; separated here.

51. The power of a test is defined as:

  1. The probability of a Type I error
  2. The probability of a Type II error
  3. The probability of rejecting H₀ when it is false
  4. The probability of failing to reject H₀ when it is true
Answer: C. The probability of rejecting H₀ when it is false
The source concatenated all options into A; separated here.

52. If the significance level α is increased (e.g., from 0.01 to 0.05), the power of the test will:

  1. Increase
  2. Decrease
  3. Stay the same
  4. Cannot be determined
Answer: A. Increase
Holding sample size, test design, variability and alternative effect fixed.

53. A test has power 0.90 when μ = 15. This means:

  1. The probability of rejecting H₀ when μ = 15 is 0.90
  2. The probability that μ = 15 is 0.90
  3. The probability of a Type I error is 0.90
  4. The probability of failing to reject H₀ when μ = 15 is 0.90
Answer: A. The probability of rejecting H₀ when μ = 15 is 0.90

54. A test has power 0.80 when μ = 8 and power 0.95 when μ = 10. This indicates that:

  1. The test is better at detecting a larger departure from H₀
  2. The test is better at detecting a smaller departure from H₀
  3. The power does not depend on the alternative value of μ
  4. The test has a Type I error rate of 0.20
Answer: A. The test is better at detecting a larger departure from H₀
Intended answer assumes μ = 10 is farther from the null mean than μ = 8. The null mean is omitted from the question.

55. A test has a Type I error probability of 0.05 and a Type II error probability of 0.20. The power of the test is:

  1. 0.05
  2. 0.20
  3. 0.80
  4. 0.95
Answer: C. 0.80

Factors affecting power

56. For a fixed sample size and significance level, power is highest when:

  1. The alternative mean is close to the null mean
  2. The alternative mean is far from the null mean
  3. The population standard deviation is large
  4. The test is two-tailed rather than one-tailed
Answer: B. The alternative mean is far from the null mean

57. If the significance level α is decreased from 0.05 to 0.01, the power of the test will:

  1. Increase
  2. Decrease
  3. Stay the same
  4. Cannot be determined
Answer: B. Decrease
Holding other factors fixed.

58. A one-tailed test and a two-tailed test use the same data with α = 0.05. The one-tailed test has:

  1. Lower power than the two-tailed test
  2. Higher power than the two-tailed test (when the effect is in the specified direction)
  3. The same power as the two-tailed test
  4. No power
Answer: B. Higher power than the two-tailed test (when the effect is in the specified direction)

59. A researcher wants to reduce both Type I and Type II error probabilities simultaneously. The most effective approach is to:

  1. Increase α and decrease the sample size
  2. Decrease α and increase the sample size
  3. Increase both α and the sample size
  4. Decrease both α and the sample size
Answer: B. Decrease α and increase the sample size
Increase the sample size enough to offset the loss in power from lowering α. The source concatenated all options into A; separated here.
